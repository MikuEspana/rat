### What this means

- **Jupiter stays on the Free tier.** The busiest minute had 40 calls, under the budget of 40 and far under Jupiter's 60. One `/swap/v2/build` per hire plus one `/price/v3` call every 45 s for every mint. When the budget is used up, hires wait for the next loop (the job-fair line); nothing fails and nothing retries in a tight loop. A 429 stops every Jupiter call with an exponential backoff (5 s up to 5 min) and alerts the owner.
- **The budget is a little tighter than the hourly cap.** Two 20-hire loops can fall inside one minute, so the budget caps hiring at about 38 builds a minute; with the price calls that tops out a little under the 60 SOL/h cap's pace in a long rush.
- **Every fee still hires rats.** 180 SOL of fees became 6,084 rats, and the hire budget was fully spent by minute 189. At the peak about 2,051 rats' worth of SOL waited; no claimed SOL waited more than 64 min for its rat.
- **The site** must handle 6,084 rats: `/api/rats` is 3321.2 KB (824.6 KB gzipped). Fetch it once, then follow `/api/events`. The building has desks for about 5,800; the rest line up outside (the job-fair line).
- Not modelled: real Jupiter routes and xStock liquidity, real priority fees, real RPC latency (the worker loop time above is in memory).
